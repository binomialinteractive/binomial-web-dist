How to play

What is
Colossal Cores

Two teams of up to eight players, red and blue, split a map of seven zones. On top of each hill stands a temple, and inside it a colossus holding up a master core. That core is the key to the match: you capture it by shooting it, and you keep it by stopping the other team from doing the same.

There's no break between attacking and defending. The zone you just took starts producing gold the moment it's yours, and that's exactly why the other team is coming to take it back. Ten minutes, seven zones and no capture is ever final.

In short

  • Capture. Shoot a colossus's master core to claim its zone.
  • Defend. If the enemy team reaches that core and damages it, the zone changes hands. No capture is permanent.
  • Earn. Every 30 seconds, each player gets 5 gold for every zone their team holds at that moment.
  • Fortify. Every captured zone spawns its own guards and turrets at level 1. Spend gold to upgrade them to levels 2 and 3: more health, more damage, better accuracy.
  • Spend. Gold also buys weapons and consumables from the vending machines in your team's base.
  • Up to eight vs. eight, ten minutes. Not a second longer.

Through the air or along the ground

Swing up to the temples on your grappler and cover ground fast on your turbo boots.

  • Grappler

    Fire, hook on and swing: it's what makes the map play vertically.

  • Turbo boots

    Full-speed sliding and a slow-motion mode.

The island

The map
shapes the strategy

In Colossal Cores the map isn't scenery: it's the essence of the game. Where the temples stand, what lies between them and how far your base is decide where you attack, what you defend and when you can afford to head back for gear.

Tap the markers to see what each spot is

Seven pillars, a colossus on each

The two teams' bases sit at opposite ends, and the seven temples are laid out between them in a symmetrical, radial pattern, with a strip of water splitting the map into two halves. The central pillar is the tallest, and the most strategic of the seven.

High up and in plain sight

The temples stand high and have no walls. Getting there is hard, but once you're up you're exposed: the only thing blocking line of sight from one temple to another is the cliffs around them. Capturing a zone means climbing to a spot where everyone can see you.

Building is not optional

Every hill is surrounded by water, so you don't just run up: you grapple or you build. And building carries on once you're on top. The core you just captured has to be fortified, because the temple alone won't protect it.

Your base is far away

The vending machines are in each team's base, far from the temples. Every trip for weapons or consumables is time away from the fight, so stocking up is about picking your moment: before you go for a zone, not in the middle of a firefight.

Mechanics

How a match
is won

Every match is a race against the clock and against the rival team's lead. Seven zones between two teams force you to choose all the time: fortify the ones you already hold, or go for another and leave your rear exposed.

Try different strategies and take control of every zone.

Capture and defend

7

zones in play at once

Shoot a colossus's master core to make its zone yours. From then on that zone is a target for the other team: if they reach the core and damage it, it changes hands. Attacking and defending aren't two phases of the match. They're the same thing, happening at once in seven different places.

The gold economy

5 gold

Five gold coins every 30 seconds, per player and per zone

Every 30 seconds, each player receives 5 gold for every zone their team holds. That gold can't be bought with real money and is only spent at the six vending machines in your base, so every purchase is a decision: heading back for gear takes you off the front, and every second away is worth zones.

Three defense levels

1 → 3

more health, more damage, more accuracy

The moment you capture a zone, guards spawn to patrol it and turrets open fire on anyone who gets close, at level 1. Gold upgrades them to levels 2 and 3: more health, more damage, better accuracy. That's the call: a level 3 zone holds on its own while you attack another, but that gold isn't in your weapon.

Zones can be lost

3 → 1

and every build is destroyed

When the enemy team takes a zone from you, an explosion destroys every structure built on it and its defenses drop back to level 1. You don't just lose the core: you lose everything you invested in protecting it. That's why fortifying well isn't a bonus. It's the only way to avoid starting from scratch.
